# The Job Market

A CNS fellowship is a fixed-term position, so the search for the next one starts well
before it feels urgent. Academic hiring runs on a long and largely invisible calendar:
faculty postings cluster in the autumn, shortlists form months later, and the gap between
sending an application and hearing anything is measured in seasons. Industry timelines are
compressed by comparison — weeks rather than months — which means the two searches
interleave badly if you start them at the same time.

The materials differ more than people expect. A faculty application is a research programme
argument: the statement has to describe work that is yours, fundable, and distinct from
your supervisor's. An industry application is a claim about capability, and the parts of
this program that read as most valuable there — pipelines, containers, reproducible
analysis at scale, working with governed clinical data — are often the parts a scholar
undersells because they feel like infrastructure rather than science.

What a fellowship of this kind signals is worth being deliberate about: independent
funding, a named methodological specialization, and experience with open data governance.
Those are concrete things to point at.
