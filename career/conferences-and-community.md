# Conferences and Community

Conferences do two different jobs and it is worth being clear which one you are there for.
The talks are the visible part and the least important; the abstracts are usually online
beforehand. What a conference actually provides is a compressed window in which people who
read your work are in one room and willing to talk to you.

For this field the main venues are **OHBM** for human brain mapping, **SfN** for
neuroscience broadly, and the disease- or method-specific meetings — AAIC, ISMRM,
MICCAI — depending on where your work sits. **Brainhack** is a different format worth
knowing about: collaborative project work rather than presentations, and unusually
effective at turning a name you recognize into someone you have actually built something
with. Several people in this book teach at [Brainhack School](../external.md).

Posters are undervalued. A talk is fifteen minutes of one-way transmission; a poster is
two hours of conversation with people who chose to stop, and it produces more collaborations
per unit effort than any other format. The failure mode is designing it like a paper —
dense, complete, and unreadable at two metres. One finding, legible from a distance, with
the detail held in reserve for the conversation.
